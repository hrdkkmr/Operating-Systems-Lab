A short write-up identifying the strategy used and why it prevents deadlock.

The program models five processes competing for three shared resource types: two printers, one scanner, and two disks.
Each process requires two resources to perform its work. Semaphores are used to control access to the resource pools,
ensuring that the number of processes using a resource never exceeds the number of available instances.

The deadlock-prevention strategy used is resource ordering. A global ordering is defined as:
Printer < Scanner < Disk

Every process acquires its required resources according to this order. For example,
a process requiring a printer and disk acquires the printer first and the disk second,
while a process requiring a scanner and disk acquires the scanner first and the disk second.

This prevents circular wait, which is one of the necessary conditions for deadlock.
Since every process follows the same resource ordering,
a process can never hold a higher-ordered resource while waiting for a lower-ordered resource.
Therefore, a circular chain of waiting processes cannot form.

The program runs all five processes for multiple cycles. Processes may temporarily block when a resource is unavailable,
but once another process releases the resource, the waiting process can continue.
The output shows that all five processes complete all cycles and release their resources without any process becoming permanently blocked.
